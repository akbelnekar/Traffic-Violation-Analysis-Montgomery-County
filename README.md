# Traffic-Violation-Analysis-Montgomery-County

## Project Overview

This project analyzes traffic violations in Montgomery County, Maryland, supporting the state's Vision Zero initiative to eliminate traffic-related deaths by 2030. The study provides insights into traffic violation patterns, accident hotspots, and potential racial disparities in traffic enforcement.

## Key Objectives

- Identify high-frequency violation areas
- Analyze temporal patterns of violations
- Examine the relationship between driver negligence and accidents
- Investigate trends in violation types
- Assess potential racial disparities in traffic stops, searches, and arrests

## Data Analysis

The team processed and cleaned data from the Montgomery government website, focusing on:

- Identifying necessary data processing approaches
- Understanding column data types
- Handling missing data based on research-driven assumptions
- Removing irrelevant columns
- Conducting consistency checks

## Key Findings

### Driver Negligence and Accidents

One out of three accidents is caused by one of six irresponsible driver actions, indicating a need for targeted traffic calming measures. These six irresponsible driver actions are:
1. Speeding
2. Distracted driving (e.g., using electronic devices)
3. Drunk driving or driving under the influence
4. Reckless or aggressive driving
5. Running red lights and stop signs
6. Fatigue or drowsy driving

### Accident Hotspots

Clusters of accidents were identified in:
- Glenmont
- White Oak
- Bethesda
- Silver Spring

These clusters are primarily located around intersections and main road junctions.

### Temporal Patterns

The analysis revealed peak hours for accidents, which correlate with rush hour traffic and driver fatigue.

### Racial Disparities

- Black and Hispanic drivers were more likely to be stopped and searched compared to White drivers.
- The most common search reason across all races was "Incident to Arrest."
- White drivers had a higher rate of contraband found during searches (57.89%) compared to Black (49.46%) and Hispanic (49.51%) drivers.

## Recommendations

1. **Koper Curve Theory**: Implement random patrols in hotspot locations for 15-20 minutes, potentially reducing crashes by 22.56%.

2. **Traffic Calming (TC)**:
   - Level 1: Educational strategies
   - Level 2: Implementation of traffic control devices
   This approach reduced crashes by 14% in New York City.

3. **Increase Seat Belt Fines**: 95% of accident victims in Montgomery County were not wearing seatbelts during impact.

4. **Data-Driven Resource Allocation**: Use analysis results for efficient deployment of law enforcement and safety resources.

5. **Road Design Re-evaluation**: Encourage safer driver behavior through improved road design.

6. **Promote Transit-Oriented Community**: Reduce reliance on personal automobiles.

## Conclusion

As normal work patterns resume, increased traffic is expected, potentially leading to more accidents and fatalities. Implementing the recommended measures is crucial for addressing driver negligence and enhancing public safety. The insights from this analysis provide a targeted approach to prevent crashes and progress towards the goal of zero traffic-related deaths.
